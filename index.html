About

I'm a PhD candidate in Geography and the Environment at the University of Oxford, where I study how agricultural support policies trade off production, the environment and rural welfare.

I'm also a consultant at Mode Economics, a climate adaptation and resilience economics advisory. I have eight years of experience in environmental and climate economics, working at Vivid Economics, McKinsey & Company, the World Economic Forum and the National Audubon Society.

Research

Works in Progress

Agricultural Support and Its Trade-offs: Distribution, Risk and Resilience (PhD thesis, University of Oxford)

Governments spend heavily supporting farmers, but these policies are usually judged on one outcome at a time. My thesis studies how agricultural support trades off gains and losses across groups: producers, consumers and the environment. It asks how support shapes farmers' production and land-use decisions by raising their income and by reducing their exposure to risk, how subsidies compare with crop insurance, and whether policies such as public food reserves build resilience to weather shocks.

Completed Research

Restoring Natural Fire and the Cost of Suppression in American Wilderness: Evidence from a Policy Experiment (MSc dissertation, LSE, 2019)

A century of total fire suppression in the American West left forests overgrown and fires more severe and costly. Under wildland fire use (WFU), naturally ignited fires in Wilderness Areas are monitored and allowed to burn as fuel treatment rather than put out. This dissertation estimates the effect of that policy on the suppression cost of later fires that overlap previously burned areas, using ignition cause and daily weather conditions as instrumental variables for treatment. Planned treatment under WFU reduced total and per-hectare suppression costs, though not significantly, while unplanned wildfire produced significant cost reductions, suggesting WFU applied at larger scale could do the same. Effects on fire intensity and fuel availability were inconclusive.
